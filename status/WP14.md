## Decisions made (and why)
- **Reading ruling C** (accepted by the lead). I read "a window that is already found" as "the window
  matches the term *without* that edge word". Such an edge word may gain anything above 0; any other edge word needs 0.05.
  - This passes all three of your cases: "bell" +0.034 accepted; "the" −0.13 and "at" −0.087 rejected.
  - It keeps the first review's case: "bell moran" is 0.737, and "thank" would lift it over 0.75 with a
    gain of only 0.013.
  - Taken literally (any window ≥ 0.75, any gain > 0), "bell moran thank" becomes the name, and "…bell
    moran, thank you all." loses its `END_INSERTION` fail.
- **Letter overlap at edges.** An edge word that shares letters with the term's edge can still join a
  window, because it raises the match (a "the" before a name that begins "th"). Only that one word is
  hidden; the other words of an inserted run stay errors.
- **F1: what counts as an extra.** An inner word is an extra when taking it out does not lower the whole
  window's ratio (gain ≤ 0), each word tested against the whole window. Among found windows, ties go to
  the fewest extras, then the fewest words. A word that is part of the name ("vesa" in "kurdell vesa
  trimens", "a" in "quill a stre") raises the ratio, so it is never an extra.
- **Money is split after the normaliser, not before.** The normaliser itself writes "three dollars and
  fifty cents" as $3.50. Splitting only the raw text would have made that phrase and a transcript's
  "$3.50" differ. Split on the output, all of "£3.50", "$3.50", "three pounds fifty" and "three dollars
  and fifty cents" read alike.
- **Minus.** Ruling D names the hyphen-minus; I also read the minus sign (U+2212) the same way. A hyphen
  inside a word or a range stays a hyphen: "1990-1995", "COVID-19".
- **Clock times.** Only one or two digits, a colon and exactly two digits are joined ("4:30", "12:05"), so
  "3:2" is left alone.
- **Earlier decisions stand:**
  - term windows read by the reader, compared on letters and digits;
  - edge contiguity for a term not found;
  - exact spans read whole tokens and drop apostrophes on both sides;
  - words in parentheses are read;
  - `EXACT_SPAN_MISMATCH` details are {cue, words, expected, heard, match}; contracts 1.5 has the assembler
    add `start`/`end`.
- **ASSUME** (to be measured in WP40): bleed ratio 0.75 with at least 2 words or 8 letters, and
  `_MIN_GAIN` 0.05. QA's matching rules are versioned by `QA_PROFILE` (confirmed by the lead).

## Known limits (for WP40 to measure; not fixed, by the lead's ruling)
- **F2. A tail insertion that completes a truncated name is absorbed.** "…velmoran. The end now." takes
  the window "velmoran the" for the term, so the take gets an `END_INSERTION` warning instead of a fail.
- **F4. Written forms reader @2 does not read as spoken:**
  - "4:30:15" vs "four thirty fifteen";
  - "0:30" vs "zero thirty";
  - "10.30" (a British clock time) vs "ten thirty";
  - "50¢" vs "fifty cents".
- **Found with F3:** "a ninety degree turn" reads `90 degree`, while "a 90° turn" reads `90 degrees`, so an
  exact span over it fails on a perfect take. The plural is what "5°" is read as most often.
- **"Oz of vine" still fails** (3 errors in 32 words, `wer_adj` 0.094). "oz vine" matches "Ossavine" at only
  0.714, so the name is not found, and "oz" gains only +0.048 at the edge, under `_MIN_GAIN`. Even with
  "oz" admitted it would be 2 errors (0.0625), still a fail. A pronunciation this far off is a real
  error, so I left it.
