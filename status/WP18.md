### 3. `tests/material` on the service's own code

The local stand-in is removed. That covers canonical form, words, the four checks, markup, spans, the
negation list, and the retyped flag codes, severities, id pattern and limits. In its place:

- every spoken-form segment:
  - is validated against `submit_job`'s published Segment schema;
  - is planned with `TextPipeline().plan_segment` and must raise **no finding, info included**;
  - must match its recorded `spoken_chars`;
- the canary's design and gate texts, and every QA spec's render text, are planned as one-cue segments,
  with the same rules;
- the canary's description must give `narration.lint.lint(...).findings == ()`;
- word edges and number words come from `narration.text.words`;
- everything else is taken from `narration.contracts`:
  - codes and severities from `codes.FLAGS` (a spec's severity must be one its code may take);
  - `names.SEVERITIES`, `MaterialStatus`, `TextWarningKind`, `CORPUS` and `BENCHMARK`;
  - the length limits from the published `design_voice` schema;
- the demo's hints go through `plan_request`: each must be applied, and no term may be split across cues;
- a new test checks that the reference-bleed spec plants the tail of the canary's design text.

The cross-checks of the text and lint fixtures against local rules are dropped, because `tests/text` and
`tests/lint` run those fixtures through the pipeline and the lint. The fixtures' well-formedness checks
remain, and the new cases join `REQUIRED_TEXT_CASES`. A quick probe confirmed the helpers catch each
written-form kind: a digit, a symbol, a unit, a lone letter, a negation, and a span that cuts a word.

## Decisions made (and why)
- ~~**Local text rules in the test file**~~ (replaced in the follow-ups by `narration.text` and `narration.lint`) (canonical form, words, the four §9.1 checks, markup, spans, the
  §3.5 word list), clearly marked as a stand-in: WP10 is not built. **When WP10 lands, switch
  `tests/material` to `narration.text` / `narration.lint`.** The local unit list is stricter than needed:
  it excludes only the English words the corpus uses (in, am, as, at).
- **Paragraphs are submit-ready Segments** (`segment_id` + `cues` of `{text, exact}`) plus metadata
  fields (`spoken_chars`, `target_spoken_chars`, `boundaries`) that a caller leaves out. Hints are
  request-level, as in §7.2.
- **Spans cover number words only, never their units**, and the corpus avoids years and clock times such
  as "a quarter past eight", to keep a false `EXACT_SPAN_MISMATCH` (which fails a ladder rung) as unlikely
  as possible. Two hour phrases remain in ladder-500 (see Questions).
- **Alignment boundary rule**: `pause` is true iff the cue ends a sentence; no cue ends in a comma or dash,
  where a pause is uncertain. The tests enforce it.
- **Canary: two seeds.** `voice.seed` designs the clip (DC-3); `gate.seed` is for the gate render. §6's
  EngineProfile records one canary `seed`; whether the gate uses `gate.seed` or the §10.3 derivation is
  WP32's call (said in `canary.json`'s notes).
- **Lint fixtures (§3.5)** added beyond the brief: cheap, needed by WP10, and they validate the local
  negation check the canary test relies on.
- **No generator script is tracked**: the JSON is the source of truth. I authored with a gitignored
  scratch script; the README explains how to edit by hand, and the tests print the expected values.
- Set folder names as briefed (`text-v1`, `qa-faults-v1`), although the other sets use `.v1`.
- Test names use the `s3_2` suffix (AGENTS.md as corrected on main).
- ~~`FLAG_CODES` is copied from §14 in the test~~: the follow-ups take the codes from `narration.contracts.codes`.
