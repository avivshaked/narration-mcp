## Decisions made (and why)
- **A cue whose only tokens are wildcards is `no_alignable_words`** (unplaced, null times, no retake).
  KNOW, from `wildcard.json`:
  - a wildcard's span edges are off by 24.5 and 32 frames at p95 (0.5–0.64 s), and by up to 51 frames,
    against the greedy reading of the words it stands for. A letter word is off by 0 frames at p95;
  - a wildcard planted where nothing was left out still takes a span. In a pause between sentences it
    takes 1 frame and scores 1.0 in 20 of 24 cases. Between two words inside a sentence it takes up to 23
    frames, scores 0.86 at the median, and pushes its neighbours by up to 54 frames;
  - real wildcards score 0.63 at the median, so a wildcard's score cannot tell spoken from unspoken.
- **The thresholds are ASSUME starting values**, from spike (b)'s calibration. `unplaced_below` is 0.50
  and `low_confidence_below` is 0.75. The calibration ran before DC-11. The wildcard moves letters only
  where they were displaced, so I BELIEVE the values still hold. The evidence tests, run with the
  wildcard, check that every spoken cue scores at least 0.6 and the median at least 0.85.
- **Method id kept:** `ctc-snap/<model>@<revision>+p<12 hex>`. The hash covers the snap settings and all
  three thresholds. So a threshold change gives a new method id, and **the alignment benchmark must be
  re-run for the new id. That is WP38's concern.** The note is in `core.py`'s `method_id` docstring.
- The decisions from the first review stand:
  - how `align_as` parts are shared out;
  - the cross-check's interval distance, with names left out;
  - emission failures are `INTERNAL`, not `ALIGNMENT_ERROR`;
  - `AlignOp` is a component, and the aligner always runs on the CPU.

### Proposed design wording (for the lead to apply at merge)
§11.2 **step 1**, replacing "A token with characters outside the alphabet, a digit or symbol left in, is
already a text warning; it is left out of the transcript, and the words around it still align." with:

> A word with characters outside the alphabet, a digit or symbol left in, is already a text warning. It
> cannot be spelled, but its speech is in the audio, so each run of such words inside one cue becomes one
> wildcard token `*` (DC-11), mapped to the run's first word. The worker aligns `*` through one extra
> emission column, log(1 − P(blank)) per frame, so it absorbs speech but not silence, and the words
> around it keep their places. A run never crosses a cue boundary. A word of a hinted term that
> `align_as` gives no letters gets no wildcard, because the term's other words spell its speech. A cue
> with no letter to place, whose words are all wildcards or which has none, is unplaced (step 7,
> `no_alignable_words`): a wildcard's span is neither precise enough to time a cue nor evidence that the
> cue was spoken.
