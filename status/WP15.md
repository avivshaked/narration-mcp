## Done
### The review's findings (one commit each, each with a regression test that failed before the fix)
- **F1, a cue swallowing an unplaced cue's speech.** An edge next to unaligned speech it does not
  own (an unplaceable cue's, including the cues before the first placed cue and after the last) now
  snaps only to a pause within reach of its own span: at most `snap_reach_start_s` (0.12 s) of speech
  between the pause and the cue's first aligned letter, or `snap_reach_end_s` (0.28 s) between its last
  and the pause. Otherwise it keeps the aligner's time, and the boundary gets `CUE_BOUNDARY_NO_PAUSE`
  (info) with `details` {`next_cue`, `edge` (`end`: the flag's cue kept its end; `start`: the next cue kept
  its start), `speech_s` (the speech between the span and the nearest pause, null with no pause)}.
  - Tests: the reviewer's three cases (middle, as `no_alignable_words` and as `low_confidence`; head;
    tail), and the reach's edge (0.12 s snaps, 0.14 s does not).
  - Why a reach, not "no speech frames at all": the aligner's letters usually lie tens of milliseconds
    inside the speech, not on the pause edge.
    KNOW (spike (b) part 4, `spikes/b-forced-align-cpu/reach.py` and `reach.json`, 24 pairs, every cue
    placed): the pause before a cue ends 0–0.10 s before its first letter (n 114, p95 0.08 s), and the
    pause after it starts −0.04–0.26 s after its last letter (n 95, p95 0.16 s). With zero reach almost no
    edge next to an unplaced cue would snap. The reaches are that maximum plus one energy frame, ASSUME
    until WP38.
  - The file's own edges (before cue 0, after the last cue) are not limited: speech there is not in the
    text, and head and end insertions (§11.1 step 7) look for it. A cue that ends on a wildcard lies
    0.32 s from its pause at the median (the wildcard misses part of the number's speech), so next to an
    unplaced cue it will often keep its wildcard end. Noted in the spike README.
  - KNOW: on the evidence tests' 24 pairs every cue time, word time, confidence and flag is identical
    before and after the whole review's fixes (the saved worker replies resolved with the old and the new
    code, byte-identical output).
- **F2, any `ok: false` reply became `ALIGNMENT_ERROR`**. Only `code == "ALIGNMENT_ERROR"` is a
  verdict now; its flag carries the worker's details. Any other code (`INTERNAL`, `GPU_OOM`,
  `NOT_LOADED`, …, or no error at all) raises `narration.contracts.errors.WorkerFailure` with the code,
  message and details, as `WorkerClient.request` does.
- **F3, a broken snapshot replied `INTERNAL`**. KNOW (transformers 5.17.0, torch 2.11, measured
  on damaged copies): a missing file or unreadable `config.json` raises `OSError`; garbage in
  `pytorch_model.bin` raises `UnpicklingError`; a truncated one `RuntimeError` (PytorchStreamReader); a
  damaged `model.safetensors` `SafetensorError`; an unreadable `vocab.json` `JSONDecodeError`. All of them
  are now `BACKEND_NOT_INSTALLED` with "install the models again (narration-admin install)". A file
  another process holds is `INTERNAL` with `details.transient`, as WP20's qwen3 worker classifies it. A
  `RuntimeError` about memory still propagates (`INTERNAL`). The tests build a tiny real CTC snapshot in
  both weight formats as the positive control and damage it six ways.
- **F4, the method id missed the wildcard and the rules**. `method_params` now also carries
  `wildcard_column` (`log1m_blank@1e-6`), `transcript_rules` (`narration.align.transcript/1`) and
  `resolve_rules` (`narration.align.resolve/1`, which covers the snapping rule and its reach). The reaches
  are `AlignerParams` fields, so they are in the id too. A server test loads the worker's module and checks
  that its wildcard column, wildcard token and alphabet are the server's.

### The nits
- `weights_only=True` is passed explicitly to `from_pretrained` (a test spies on the call).
- The vocabulary's ids must be exactly 0..N−1 (ints, no gap, none twice), so the wildcard's id N cannot
  collide; its size must equal `config.json`'s `vocab_size`; and it must hold the blank and the alphabet.
- **A non-CTC model is refused twice.** I chose both options, because they guard different mistakes:
  - the worker refuses, at load, a snapshot whose `config.json` does not name `Wav2Vec2ForCTC` (the Qwen
    forced aligner, a classifier), with `BACKEND_NOT_INSTALLED`: the directory named by the pinned revision
    holds the wrong files, which is an install fault, and the design's code for "weights missing";
  - `CtcAligner` (and so `from_config`) refuses a model outside `CTC_MODELS` (the pinned wav2vec2 model)
    with a `ValueError` that says what to set: `ctc-snap`'s thresholds were measured for that model only,
    and the Qwen aligner would be another method, not a relabelled `ctc-snap`.
- Confidence counts letters and apostrophes, not `|` or `*`: the docstrings say so, and the proposed
  step 5 wording below does.

## Decisions made (and why)
- **The lead's decisions on the reviewer's questions:**
  1. A cue whose edge word cannot be spelled takes that edge from the wildcard's span when no pause snaps
     it. Accepted for v1; in the proposed step 1 wording below.
  2. Narrowing the wildcard's column to letter mass only: not now. It would need re-measuring under a new
     method id. A possible later change (under Next).
  3. An `align_as` with characters outside the alphabet silently becoming a wildcard: out of WP15's
     scope; an open question for the text owner (WP10), below.
  4. The thresholds stay ASSUME, and so do the two new reaches.
  5. F5 (emission memory beyond about 40 s in one pass) is measured later by WP22/WP38 (under Next).
- **F2 raises `WorkerFailure`, not `QaUnavailable`.** It keeps the worker's code, so the caller can tell
  `GPU_OOM` (a segment error of its own in §14) from the rest, which become `QA_UNAVAILABLE`; and it is
  exactly what `WorkerClient.request` raises for the same reply, so the caller handles one exception
  whichever way the reply reached it.
- **F1 applies the reach only where the cue does not own the speech beyond its edge.** Where the cue
  owns unaligned words (a transcript without wildcards, or a hinted term's absorbed word), the pause
  beyond them is its own, and the earlier rule stands. Between two placed cues with nothing unaligned
  between them, the longest pause stands (unchanged).
- The decisions of the earlier rounds stand (the wildcard-only cue is `no_alignable_words`; emission
  failures are `INTERNAL`; `AlignOp` is a component; the aligner runs on the CPU only).

### Proposed design wording (for the lead to apply at merge)
§11.2 **step 1**, replacing "A token with characters outside the alphabet, a digit or symbol left in, is
already a text warning; it is left out of the transcript, and the words around it still align." with:

> A word with characters outside the alphabet, a digit or symbol left in, is already a text warning. It
> cannot be spelled, but its speech is in the audio, so each run of such words inside one cue becomes one
> wildcard token `*` (DC-11), mapped to the run's first word. The worker aligns `*` through one extra
> emission column, log(1 − P(blank)) per frame, floored at 1e-6, so it absorbs speech but not silence, and
> the words around it keep their places. A run never crosses a cue boundary. A word of a hinted term that
> `align_as` gives no letters gets no wildcard, because the term's other words spell its speech. A cue
> with no letter to place, whose words are all wildcards or which has none, is unplaced (step 7,
> `no_alignable_words`): a wildcard's span is neither precise enough to time a cue nor evidence that the
> cue was spoken. A cue whose first or last word is a wildcard takes that edge from the wildcard's span
> when no pause snaps it (v1; the span may miss part of the word's speech).

**Step 3:** "(BELIEVED to run on this build's CPU; Phase 0 re-checks and saves it)" becomes "(KNOW: they
run on this build's CPU; spike (b))".

**Step 4**, adding after "The first onset and last offset are snapped the same way.":

> A cue never takes speech it does not own. Next to an unplaceable cue's speech (including the cues
> before the first placed cue and after the last), an edge snaps only to a pause within reach of its own
> aligned words: at most `snap_reach_start_s` (0.12 s, ASSUME) of speech between the pause and the cue's
> first letter, or `snap_reach_end_s` (0.28 s, ASSUME) between its last letter and the pause (spike (b)
> part 4 measured at most 0.10 s and 0.26 s where the edge is the cue's own). Otherwise the edge keeps
> the CTC time and `CUE_BOUNDARY_NO_PAUSE` is added with `details.edge`.

**Step 5:** "The mean token posterior per cue" becomes "The mean posterior of the cue's letters and
apostrophes (not `|`, not `*`)".

**Step 8:** "The hash covers every setting the aligner decides by (the pause and snap parameters and the
thresholds)" becomes "The hash covers every setting the aligner decides by (the pause and snap
parameters, the reaches and the thresholds), the wildcard's column, and the versions of the transcript's
and the resolver's rules".

## Questions for the lead / owner
1. **WP14's fallback `CUE_UNALIGNED`** (`narration.qa.checks.alignment_flags`, for a cue with null times
   that the aligner did not flag) carries no `details.reason`. DC-12 asks for a reason on every unplaced
   cue, for example `missing`. `narration.align` always flags its own unplaced cues with a reason, so with
   this aligner the fallback should not fire.
2. **Private names already on main:** `src/narration/text/hints.py` and `tests/text/test_hints_s9_1.py`
   (WP10) use one of the story's invented names, which `docs/design.md` cites. I left them alone.
3. **WP22:** once it packages `workers/qa` and registers the `qa` entry point, the `sys.path` adapter in
   `workers/qa/tests/conftest.py` can go, and so can the evidence test's `PYTHONPATH`. `QaHandler` should
   own an `AlignOp`, as its docstring shows.
4. **WP36:** pass `reply=None` only for an `ALIGNMENT_ERROR` reply (`error=` its details) or when your own
   guard fails (`error=aligner.guard_details(...)`). Any other `ok: false` reply makes `resolve` raise
   `WorkerFailure`; record `QA_UNAVAILABLE` (or the code's own segment error).
5. **For WP10 (the text owner):** an `align_as` with characters outside the aligner's alphabet (a digit, a
   symbol) makes that term's word a wildcard, silently. Should `check_text` warn about such an `align_as`,
   or refuse it?
6. **Consistency with WP20:** the qwen3 worker answers another model's snapshot with `INVALID_REQUEST`
   (`field` `model.snapshot_dir`); the aligner answers `BACKEND_NOT_INSTALLED`, as the review suggested
   and as §14 describes a missing model ("weights ... missing"). Both are defensible; the lead may want
   one rule for every worker.
7. **`AlignRequest`'s location:** the authorisation named interfaces.py, but the class is in
   `narration_worker.protocol`; the docstring change is there (above). Drop that hunk if you did not mean it.
