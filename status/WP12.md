## Decisions made (and why)
- The first round's decisions stand, except where the review changed them:
  - relative sidecar paths;
  - measurement folders named by hex;
  - the first publish of a key wins;
  - one measurement per (voice, engine profile);
  - `create_job` dedupe, now with DC-6;
  - the `analysis_key` hint set;
  - candidate folders;
  - `put_profile(None)`;
  - re-entrant claims;
  - `update_job` NOT_FOUND;
  - a 1-day grace period;
  - `synchronous=FULL`.
- **gc holds the write lock while it renames.** The renames are fast. A path that stays locked costs up
  to about 1 s of retries before it is listed as an error and skipped. gc is an operator command.
- **A leftover `.trash-` name** (a removal that failed after the commit) is collected as a leftover once
  it is past the grace period.
- **Returning the caller's audio makes it writable again**, as the caller had it before `move_into` made
  it read-only.
- **The DC-6 hint** is a sentence, as other hints are: "Use a new key for a different request."

Decisions:
- The batch bounds are module constants, not configuration. They bound lock hold time; they are not a
  policy a user tunes.
- Each batch re-runs the plan under its lock. That costs one plan per 200 items, a few milliseconds on
  stores of thousands of items, and it keeps an item used during gc from being collected.
- `bytes` in a real run's report now counts what was actually removed. A trash name that could not be
  removed is listed under `errors` and not counted.
- The window first recorded here is closed (rows 6 and 7). gc still removes leftovers outside the lock,
  but it now judges each by when its name was made, and every work-in-progress name is younger than
  `grace_s` while its publisher can still use it. The time in a trash name is whole seconds from the
  store's clock, and only compared against a 1-day grace, so clock skew between processes of one store
  does not matter. A `.tmp-`/`.staging-` name is still judged by its mtime, which is now always the time
  it was made.
